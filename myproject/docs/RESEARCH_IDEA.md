## 1. Research Question

The project studies hydrodynamic drag generated by a composite seagrass-like structure: a rigid cylindrical stem together with three columns of flexible strips or leaves. The central scientific question is:

> Can a physics-structured neural surrogate predict total drag and expose interpretable changes in stem drag, leaf drag, shielding, and reconfiguration across flow speed, material stiffness, geometry, and initial orientation?

The physical variables of interest include flow speed `U`, Reynolds number `Re`, Cauchy number `Ca`, Young's modulus `E`, strip dimensions, initial angles, stem dimensions, and the number of strips per column. The target is not only a force curve. The intended analysis also explains how flexible elements bend, reorient, shield downstream columns, and redistribute the total force.

## 2. Physical Data-Generation Chain

The MATLAB workflow is the source of the structured physical quantities used by the neural model:

```text
main_clean.m
  -> material and geometry configurations
  -> flow-speed sweep and experimental-data processing
  -> predictDragForces.m
  -> calculate_drag_coefficient_v2.m
  -> finite-difference Euler-Bernoulli beam iteration
  -> local loads, deformation, angles, column forces, shielding
  -> exportPINNTrainingData.m
  -> pinn_training_data.mat
```

The synthetic export follows the same MATLAB solver family through `exportSyntheticPINNTrainingData.m` and `main_export_pinn_data.m`. The current `.mat` files are MATLAB v7.3/HDF5 files. The flattened training export contains 12 configurations and 19 velocity points per configuration, while the optional synthetic file contributes 80 solver-generated samples. See [PINN_DATA_README.md](PINN_DATA_README.md) for the field contract.

The physical structure represented in the forward model is approximately:

```text
F_stem = 0.5 * rho * U^2 * Cd_stem_eff * D * H

F_leaf_columns = 0.5 * rho * U^2 * Cd_leaf_eff * h * L
                 * N_per_column * sin(theta)^2
                 * column_correction * reconfiguration_gain

F_physics = F_stem + sum(F_leaf_columns)
F_pred = F_physics + bounded_residual
```

This equation is the organizing inductive bias. It is not a claim that every exported target was generated by exactly the same algebraic expression; the physical labels and export semantics still require a final audit.

## 3. Method Positioning

The current Python entry point is `train_latent_physics_pinn.py`. Its model is best described as a **physics-structured latent neural surrogate** or a **latent-physics multi-fidelity model**.

It should not be described as a strict PDE PINN in the narrow sense. The model does not use automatic differentiation to solve the Euler-Bernoulli equation as a residual constraint during neural training. Instead, it embeds a differentiable drag decomposition and learns bounded, interpretable latent corrections around that structure.

The encoder uses engineering features and a stack of `Linear + SiLU + LayerNorm` blocks. The latent head has 10 outputs. These control or diagnose quantities such as:

- effective stem drag coefficient;
- effective flexible-leaf drag coefficient;
- shielding coefficient;
- reconfiguration factor and its gain;
- column-level corrections;
- polynomial reconfiguration terms;
- a bounded force residual.

The model is supervised by total force and selected auxiliary labels, including leaf force, column forces, and shielding-related quantities. The purpose of the latent variables is scientific interpretability and physical regularization, not to replace the measured total-force target with an unverified latent target.

## 4. AI for Science Narrative

### 4.1 Proposed story

Flexible vegetation is a multiscale fluid-structure interaction problem. Direct high-fidelity simulation or repeated beam iteration is expensive, while a purely black-box regressor can fit force data without revealing why drag changes. This project combines the two sources of information:

1. MATLAB supplies a mechanistic, geometry-aware description of bending, local loading, column shielding, and force decomposition.
2. Experiments anchor the model to observed total drag and configuration-dependent behavior.
3. A latent neural surrogate learns effective corrections that remain connected to physical quantities.
4. Synthetic solver samples expand the training support without being used as the sole measure of experimental generalization.

The intended scientific contribution is therefore not simply a lower prediction error. It is a model that turns sparse experimental force observations and a lower-fidelity physical solver into a testable, interpretable surrogate for flexible-vegetation drag.

### 4.2 Candidate contributions

- **Physics-structured prediction:** the force forward pass preserves stem, leaf, column, shielding, and reconfiguration structure.
- **Interpretable latent variables:** learned effective coefficients and correction factors can be plotted against `Re`, `Ca`, stiffness, and geometry.
- **Multi-fidelity learning:** solver-generated samples are used as training support while experimental validation remains identifiable.
- **Mechanistic diagnostics:** the model exports force decomposition, column forces, angles, shielding, and residual magnitude instead of only a scalar prediction.
- **A reusable surrogate:** once trained, the model can evaluate parameter combinations more cheaply than repeatedly running the full MATLAB iteration.

These are candidate paper claims until the ablations and independent generalization tests below are completed.

## 6. Minimum Paper Experiment Matrix

Before presenting a high-level claim, the following comparisons should be run with fixed preprocessing, multiple seeds, and confidence intervals where feasible:

1. Experimental-only versus experimental + synthetic training using the same latent architecture.
2. Full latent-physics model versus a black-box MLP with the same parameter budget.
3. Full model versus removal of shielding, reconfiguration, column correction, and bounded residual terms one at a time.
4. Auxiliary supervision on versus off for leaf force, column force, and shielding labels.
5. Random sample split versus configuration-level holdout, material-family holdout, and initial-angle holdout.
6. MATLAB synthetic targets versus any alternative synthetic weighting or pretraining schedule.
7. Seed sensitivity and hyperparameter sensitivity for the reported baseline.
8. Force-only metrics plus physical plausibility metrics for angles, shielding, column-force fractions, and residual ratios.

The current random validation split is suitable for a first engineering check but does not establish configuration extrapolation. A group or configuration holdout is required for that claim.

## 8. Physics and Data Semantics Requiring Audit

The following issues are deliberately recorded instead of being silently resolved in documentation:

- `F_exp_mean_adjusted` includes an adjustment and must be defined precisely before it is described as raw experimental force.
- The relationship between `F_total_iter`, `F_total_rigid`, and `F_total_Ca` must be traced through the MATLAB export and Python target selection.
- The solver uses a `sin^2(theta)` force structure in one path, while fallback or legacy calculations may contain `sin^3(theta)` behavior.
- Shielding may be applied at more than one stage in some MATLAB paths; double application must be ruled out.
- MATLAB default drag coefficients and legacy Python defaults are not automatically interchangeable.
- Random sample splits can place nearby velocities from the same configuration in train and validation, making validation easier than true configuration extrapolation.
- The physical-angle option and tangent-angle option are different data-generation settings and should not be pooled without a version label.

## 10. Scientific Interpretation of the Iterative Route

The iterative route is plausible as an engineering experiment, especially when experimental samples are sparse along velocity and the model needs a smoother interpolation surface. Its strongest defensible claim is that repeated low-weight distillation may improve optimization stability or regularization under a fixed architecture. Convergence of training loss or cycle predictions does not prove convergence toward the true force law.

A publication-quality evaluation must separate three questions:

1. Does self-training improve performance on an untouched experimental holdout?
2. Does any gain survive configuration/material extrapolation rather than only within-configuration velocity interpolation?
3. Does solver-labeled active learning outperform self-labeling at the same added-sample budget?

The present random row validation split addresses interpolation and cycle control only. It must not be used to claim generalization to unseen materials, geometry, initial angle, or structural stiffness.
